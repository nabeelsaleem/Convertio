Find Out How Many Pages Are in a PDF, Instantly

Counting the pages in a PDF should not require installing software or signing up for anything. This free tool tells you in seconds: drop one PDF — or a whole batch of them — into the box above, and each file's page count appears alongside a combined total.

Under the hood, the tool uses pdf.js, the same open-source PDF engine used by major browsers, to open each document and read its internal page tree. The count comes straight from the document's own structure, so it matches what you would see in any PDF reader — no guessing, no estimates.

Bulk counting is where the tool saves the most time. Print shops price jobs by page count, binding and booklet quotes depend on it, and anyone who bills per page needs exact numbers for invoices. Instead of opening dozens of files one by one, you get per-file counts plus the average pages per document and a grand total in a single pass.

PDF Page Counter

What it is: A browser-based utility that counts the pages of single or bulk PDF uploads.

When to use it: You need an exact page total for printing quotes, client invoicing, or organizing large document sets.

Why it's free: It runs entirely in your browser, so there are no server costs to pass on to you.

It works in any modern browser on desktop or phone, with nothing to install. It is also a handy sanity check before sharing: confirm a report has all its pages, or verify that an exported ebook came out complete.

Everything happens on your device. Your files are read locally and never uploaded to a server, so confidential contracts, manuscripts, and legal documents stay private.

Two practical notes: scanned PDFs count just like born-digital ones — a page is a page regardless of what is printed on it. And password-protected files cannot be opened without their password, so unlock those first.

If the total is higher than expected, the related tools below can help you act on it: split a PDF into smaller files, merge several PDFs into one, or compress a PDF before emailing it.
