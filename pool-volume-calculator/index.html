How to Calculate Pool Volume in Gallons

Knowing the exact water capacity of your swimming pool is essential before adding chlorine, shock, salt, or pH balancers, and before sizing a replacement pump or heater. Our Pool Volume Calculator computes capacity in US gallons, UK (Imperial) gallons, liters, cubic feet, and cubic meters from a single set of measurements.

To calculate pool volume manually, find the surface area of the water, multiply by the average water depth to get cubic volume, and then convert cubic feet to US gallons by multiplying by 7.4805 (or multiply cubic meters by 1,000 for liters and 264.172 for US gallons).

1. Rectangular Pool Volume Formula

For a standard rectangular or square pool, multiply length by width by average depth:

US Gallons = Length (ft) x Width (ft) x Average Depth (ft) x 7.4805
Liters = Length (m) x Width (m) x Average Depth (m) x 1,000

Worked Example (How many gallons is a 16x32 pool?): Suppose you have a 16 ft wide by 32 ft long in-ground pool with a 3.5 ft shallow end and an 8 ft deep end.

  1. Average depth: (3.5 + 8) / 2 = 5.75 ft
  2. Surface area: 32 x 16 = 512 sq ft
  3. Cubic volume: 512 x 5.75 = 2,944 cu ft
  4. Total US gallons: 2,944 x 7.4805 = 22,023 US gallons (about 83,365 liters)

2. Round Pool Gallons Formula

For circular above-ground or in-ground pools, measure the full diameter across the center of the pool and divide by 2 to get the radius ($r = d / 2$):

US Gallons = 3.14159 x Radius (ft) x Radius (ft) x Average Depth (ft) x 7.4805
Shortcut: Diameter (ft) x Diameter (ft) x Average Depth (ft) x 5.875

For example, an 18-foot round above-ground pool with 4 feet of actual water depth has a radius of 9 ft: 3.14159 x 9 x 9 x 4 x 7.4805 = 7,614 US gallons.

3. Oval Pool Volume Formula

Oval pools use the area of an ellipse ($\pi \times \text{half-length} \times \text{half-width}$):

US Gallons = 3.14159 x (Length / 2) x (Width / 2) x Average Depth x 7.4805
Shortcut: Length (ft) x Width (ft) x Average Depth (ft) x 5.875

4. Kidney & Freeform Pool Volume Formula

Kidney-shaped pools have two rounded lobes of different widths. Measure the longest overall length, the widest distance across the large lobe (Width A), and the widest distance across the smaller lobe (Width B):

Surface Area = 0.45 x (Width A + Width B) x Length
US Gallons = Surface Area (sq ft) x Average Depth (ft) x 7.4805

How to Find Average Pool Depth

If your pool has a flat bottom (common in above-ground pools), your average depth is simply the actual water depth. For sloped in-ground pools with a gradual transition from shallow to deep, add the shallowest water depth and the deepest water depth and divide by 2:

Average Depth = (Shallow End Water Depth + Deep End Water Depth) / 2

Important measurement tip: Measure from the pool floor to the actual water line (typically the middle of the skimmer opening), not to the top edge of the pool wall. A 48-inch above-ground pool wall usually holds about 42 to 44 inches (3.5 to 3.67 feet) of water. If your pool has a sharp drop-off rather than a steady slope, calculate the shallow section and deep hopper separately and add the two volumes together.

Planning Formulas: Fill Time, Weight & Pump Turnover

  • Water Weight: Fresh water weighs approximately 8.3454 lbs per US gallon (1 kg per liter).
  • Fill Time: Total volume divided by your hose flow rate (a standard 5/8-inch garden hose delivers roughly 8 to 12 GPM).
  • Fill Cost: Total volume divided by 1,000 gallons (or per cubic meter in Metric mode) multiplied by your local municipal water rate.
  • Pump Turnover Rate: Residential pools should circulate their entire water volume at least once every 8 hours, calculated as Total Volume divided by 480 minutes.

Frequently Asked Questions

A standard 16x32 foot rectangular pool holds between 19,150 and 23,000 US gallons depending on its depth. With a typical 3.5-foot shallow end and 8-foot deep end (5.75-foot average depth), a 16x32 pool holds about 22,023 US gallons (83,365 liters). With a flat 5-foot depth, it holds 19,150 US gallons.

First calculate the pool's volume in cubic feet by multiplying its surface area by its average depth, then multiply cubic feet by 7.4805 to convert to US gallons. For a rectangular pool, the formula is Length x Width x Average Depth x 7.4805. For a round pool, use 3.14159 x Radius x Radius x Average Depth x 7.4805.

For a pool with a steady, uniform slope from shallow to deep, add the shallow end water depth and the deep end water depth together and divide by 2. For example, a 3.5-foot shallow end and an 8-foot deep end give an average depth of (3.5 + 8) / 2 = 5.75 feet. Always measure actual water depth rather than wall height.

A 15-foot round pool with 4 feet of water holds about 5,288 US gallons. An 18-foot round pool at 4 feet holds about 7,614 US gallons, and a 24-foot round pool at 4.5 feet of water holds about 15,229 US gallons.

Always measure actual water depth from the pool floor to the middle of the skimmer opening, not the total wall height. Pool water usually sits 4 to 6 inches below the top coping or rim, and using wall height instead of water depth will overestimate your volume and cause chemical overdosing.

Measure the longest length and the two widest points across each lobe (Width A and Width B). Multiply 0.45 by (Width A + Width B) by Length to estimate surface area, then multiply by Average Depth and 7.4805 for US gallons.

A typical residential garden hose delivers 8 to 12 gallons per minute (480 to 720 gallons per hour). At 10 gallons per minute, filling a 15,000-gallon pool takes about 25 hours, while a 22,000-gallon pool takes around 36 to 37 hours.

Chlorine, pH adjusters, algaecides, and stabilizer dosages are calculated per 10,000 gallons (or per 10,000 liters). Knowing your exact volume prevents under-sanitizing or over-chlorinating the water and helps you size your pump and filter to achieve a full turnover every 8 hours.
