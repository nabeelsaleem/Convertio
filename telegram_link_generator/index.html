What a t.me Link Actually Does

Every public Telegram identity — your personal profile, a public channel, or a public group — can be reached through a short address on Telegram’s own t.me domain, like https://t.me/yourusername. It is the fastest bridge between anywhere on the web and a Telegram conversation. On a phone with Telegram installed, tapping the link opens the app straight to your chat or channel. On a computer, or a phone without the app, it opens a tidy web preview where the visitor can look around first and then message you. No contact exchange, no phone numbers, no friction.

The Two Links This Generator Creates

The tool above builds your link live, as you type, in exactly the format Telegram expects. Depending on what you enter, you get one of two outputs:

  • Username only → https://t.me/yourusername. A direct link to your profile, public channel, or public group. This is the link most people want for bios, signatures, and contact pages.
  • Username plus a pre-filled message → https://t.me/yourusername?text=Hello.... Your message is URL-encoded and attached as a ?text= parameter. When someone opens the link, the chat opens with your text already sitting in the message box — the visitor just presses send. A short prompt like “Hi, I’d like a quote for...” tells you instantly why they are writing, which beats a blank chat window every time.

Everything runs in your browser. The generator never asks for your password, never calls the Telegram API, and never stores your username — it simply assembles a standard URL from the text you type, the same way you could by hand.

How to Create Your Telegram Link

  1. Find your username. In Telegram, open Settings and look at the Username field (it is shown with an @). If it is empty, choose one first — the generator can only link to a username that actually exists.
  2. Type it into the Username / Handle box above. Include the @ or leave it off; the tool strips it automatically and tidies up extra spaces.
  3. Optionally, add a pre-filled message — a greeting, a question, or a one-line note about why people usually contact you.
  4. Copy your link. Use the Copy button for one-click copying, or press Test Link to open it in a new tab and confirm it lands in the right place. Reset clears both fields when you want to start over.

Privacy: Talk Without Sharing Your Number

This is the real reason t.me links exist. Telegram lets anyone message a username without ever seeing the phone number behind it. Publish the link on a public page, drop it in a group, or print it on a business card, and the only thing a stranger learns is your public handle — the same handle anyone could already find through Telegram’s own search. Your number stays visible only to people you have explicitly allowed in your privacy settings. That separation is what makes a t.me link safe to broadcast, while a raw phone number is not.

Telegram Username Rules

A link is only as good as the username behind it. Telegram usernames must be 5 to 32 characters long and may contain only Latin letters, numbers, and underscores — no spaces, no dashes. Each one is unique across all of Telegram and case-insensitive, so t.me/YourName and t.me/yourname open the same chat. If your generated link opens an error page, the usual suspects are a typo, a username you changed or deleted, or a handle that was never set. Fix it in Telegram’s settings and regenerate; the tool keeps nothing in memory, so the new link is correct immediately.

What This Tool Can’t Do

Two kinds of Telegram links need Telegram itself rather than a third-party generator:

  • Private group and channel invite links. Those are random, revocable addresses (like t.me/+AbCdEf...) created inside the group’s settings under Invite Links — only an admin can generate or revoke them, and no outside tool can do it for you.
  • Bot deep links with parameters. Addresses like t.me/MyBot?start=payload are defined by the bot’s developer through the Bot API; this generator builds the plain t.me/username form only.

For everything public — profiles, public channels, public groups — the link above is all you need.
