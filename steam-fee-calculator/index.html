Understanding the Steam Community Market Fee

Selling items on the Steam Community Market is the main way to turn in-game drops, skins, and trading cards into Steam Wallet funds. But many sellers are surprised when they receive less than the listing price — that difference is Valve's fee, deducted from every sale.

For most games the total fee is 15%, split into two parts: a 5% Steam Transaction Fee that goes to Valve, and a 10% game-specific fee that goes to the game's publisher. Titles like Counter-Strike 2, Rust, and Dota 2 all use this split, and the deduction is automatic — you never pay it separately.

How the 15% Steam Tax is Calculated

Valve calculates both fees from the seller's receive amount (what you take home), not from what the buyer pays. The buyer's price is your receive amount plus both fees, rounded up to the nearest cent.

  • Steam Transaction Fee: 5% of the seller's price (minimum $0.01).
  • Game Fee: 10% of the seller's price (minimum $0.01).
  • Total Cost: The sum of the seller's price plus both fees.

Each fee has a $0.01 minimum, so the smallest possible total fee is $0.02. That stings on cheap listings: sell a trading card for $0.03 and you keep only $0.01. For low-value items like crates and cards, fees can eat a huge share of the sale price, which is why many sellers wait for prices to rise before listing.

Tips for Selling on the Steam Market

A few habits go a long way toward getting more out of every sale:

  • Watch the market before you list. Prices swing with game updates, operations, and esports events — selling during a demand spike can easily beat the fee you pay.
  • Bundle or hold cheap items. The $0.02 minimum fee takes a huge bite out of $0.03 listings, so low-value crates and cards are often better sold in bulk or held until prices rise.
  • Compare against third-party marketplaces for expensive skins. They can pay out in real money with lower fees, but they carry scam and account risks that the official market does not.
