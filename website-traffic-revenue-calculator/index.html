How This Traffic Revenue Calculator Works

The estimate comes from one simple formula publishers have used for decades:

Monthly revenue = (monthly pageviews ÷ 1,000) × RPM

RPM stands for revenue per mille — mille being Latin for a thousand — and it tells you how many dollars you earn for every 1,000 pageviews. Plug in the numbers above and the tool shows your estimated monthly revenue, plus a daily figure (monthly ÷ 30.44) and a yearly projection (monthly × 12). The efficiency gauge under the results grades your RPM from Low to Premium, so you can see at a glance whether your niche is pulling its weight.

This is a planning estimate, not a promise. Ad networks run real-time auctions on every impression, so the number moves day to day. What the calculator gives you is a realistic range for business decisions: pricing a site for sale, deciding whether to hire writers, or judging whether it is time to graduate from AdSense to a premium network.

Session RPM vs. Pageview RPM: Don't Mix Them Up

This is the single most common source of inflated estimates. Pageview RPM is revenue per 1,000 pageviews; session RPM is revenue per 1,000 visitor sessions. Because the average session produces roughly 1.5 to 2 pageviews, session RPM is naturally much higher — a site with a $20 pageview RPM might report a $35 session RPM.

This calculator uses pageview RPM, so enter monthly pageviews (from Google Analytics' "Views" metric), not sessions. If your ad network dashboard reports session RPM, convert it before entering it here, or your projection will be roughly double the reality.

What Moves Your RPM Up or Down

Five forces decide whether your estimate lands high or low:

1. Your niche

Advertisers bid more where customers are worth more. A visitor about to buy home insurance is gold; a casual recipe browser is copper. Picking a higher-intent niche is the highest-leverage decision a publisher makes.

2. Where your readers live

Traffic from the US, UK, Canada, and Australia typically commands two to five times the rates of traffic from lower-ad-spend regions. Two blogs with identical pageviews can differ by thousands of dollars a month purely on geography.

3. Time of year

Ad spend peaks in Q4 when brands chase holiday shoppers, then drops sharply in January. Expect your RPM to swing 30–50% between December and January — normal, not a sign something broke.

4. Ad layout and viewability

Ads that load where readers actually look — above the fold, inside long articles, in sticky sidebars — earn far more than footer units nobody sees. Aim for viewability above 70%. Lazy-loading below-the-fold units also speeds up the page, which keeps readers around for more impressions.

5. Your ad network

AdSense is the easiest door in, but it is also the floor of the market. Premium networks run deeper advertiser auctions and optimize placements aggressively, often doubling or tripling RPM for qualifying sites. If you are on AdSense, run your numbers through our AdSense revenue calculator to see your current baseline before comparing.

Turning an Estimate Into Higher Earnings

An estimate is only useful if it changes what you do. Four moves reliably lift real earnings:

  • Publish higher-intent content. Comparison and review articles ("best X for Y") attract advertisers bidding on buyer keywords. They earn more per visit and open the door to affiliate income — model that with our affiliate earnings calculator.
  • Improve viewability before adding more ads. One visible ad slot beats three invisible ones. Test placements, then re-run your pageview-to-revenue math monthly with the pageviews to revenue calculator.
  • Qualify for a premium network. Most want 50,000+ monthly sessions of largely Tier-1 traffic. When you are close, the revenue jump usually justifies the stricter ad layout rules.
  • Diversify beyond display ads. Email newsletters (email revenue calculator), push notifications (push revenue calculator), and selling to your whole audience rather than per-pageview (audience revenue calculator) smooth out the Q1 dips that pure display sites suffer.

Finally, remember that traffic is only half the equation — the quality and intent of that traffic decide your take-home. Track which content earns, double down on it, and let the estimate guide the budget, not replace the judgment.
