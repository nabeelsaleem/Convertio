Understanding PNG to GIF Conversion Basics

PNG is a lossless format with full transparency support, which makes it ideal for graphics, logos, and screenshots. GIF supports animation but is limited to 256 colors per frame. Converting means packaging your PNG frames into a single animated GIF file; the tool applies dithering to keep gradients smooth despite the smaller palette.

If your source images have transparency, it carries over into the GIF. For the smallest file size, keep frames at the dimensions you actually need and avoid unnecessary detail — flat graphics and icons compress far better than photographs.

The Science Behind PNG to GIF Conversion

GIF uses LZW compression and a maximum 256-color palette. Transparency is preserved, but photographic PNGs will lose some color detail — that is where dithering helps, scattering pixels to simulate missing shades and avoid banding in gradients.

File size depends on dimensions, frame count, and color complexity. Simple graphics with few colors produce tiny GIFs; full-screen photographic animations get large fast. Reducing dimensions and frame count is the most effective way to shrink output.
